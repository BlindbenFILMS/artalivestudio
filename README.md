## Before launch: three settings

Open `site-config.js` in Notepad, fill in the blanks between the quote marks, and save. Every page reads this one file.

1. **Contact form.** Make a free account at https://formspree.io, choose New form, and copy its address (it looks like `https://formspree.io/f/abcdwxyz`). Paste it into `formEndpoint`. Every message then arrives in your email. Until you do this, the forms say "not connected yet" and send nothing.
2. **Public email (optional).** Put an address in `contactEmail` to show it on the contact page. Leave it empty to keep your email off the site.
3. **Visitor counting (optional).** Make a free account at https://www.goatcounter.com (no cookies, no banner needed). If your dashboard is `artalive.goatcounter.com`, type `artalive` into `goatcounter`. It counts page views plus these moments in the demo: camera started, watched without the camera, shape found, and forms sent.

Then in GitHub Desktop: write a short summary, click **Commit to main**, then **Push origin**. Changes are live in a minute or two.

## Your own voice (Chatterbox)

Every fixed line the site speaks (the camera help and the example games) can play in your cloned voice instead of the computer voice.

1. Put a clean 10-20 second recording of your voice in the `voice` folder, named `my-voice.wav`.
2. Open a terminal in `voice` and run `python make_voice.py` (use the Python where Chatterbox is installed).
3. Commit and push. Any line without a file keeps the computer voice, so you can do it a few at a time.

Made the files another way? Name them as in `voice/voice-lines.csv` (for example `camera-03.mp3`), put them in `voice`, and run `python make_voice.py --index`.

## Plans and automatic billing

Everyone starts on Free (3 published pieces). Paid plans switch on and off by themselves through Stripe.

1. **Database:** in Supabase, open SQL Editor, then run `setup/plans-setup.sql`, then `setup/billing-setup.sql`.
2. **Payment links:** in Stripe, make 4 Payment Links: $10, $25, $50 and $100 a month. Under "After payment", choose to redirect to `https://artalivestudio.com/plans.html?thanks=1`. Paste the links into `stripeLinks` in `site-config.js`.
3. **Payments helper:** in Supabase, open Edge Functions, choose to deploy a new function with the editor, name it `stripe-webhook`, and paste all of `setup/stripe-webhook.ts`. In the function's settings, turn **off** "Verify JWT" (Stripe doesn't sign in to Supabase).
4. **Tell Stripe:** in Stripe, open Developers → Webhooks and add an endpoint at `https://skacvtlqmbnkkyatkogn.supabase.co/functions/v1/stripe-webhook`. Choose these events: `checkout.session.completed`, `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`.
5. **The secret:** Stripe then shows a signing secret starting `whsec_`. In Supabase, open Edge Functions → Secrets and add it as `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`.
6. **Billing page (optional):** in Stripe, turn on the Customer portal and paste its login link into `stripePortal` in `site-config.js`. Paying customers use it to change plan, update their card or cancel.

When someone cancels, or Stripe gives up on a failed card, they go back to Free. Nothing is deleted: visitors see their first 3 pieces until they unpublish some or subscribe again. Your own `owner` row and any `custom` deals are never changed automatically. To give someone a custom deal, set their row in Table Editor → plans to tier `custom` and the number you agreed.

If you change a price in Stripe, change it in Table Editor → plan_prices too (amounts are in cents: 1000 is $10).

## Magic Postcards (artalivestudio.com/magicpostcard)

A postcard that comes alive. People pick a picture (our Asbury Park boardwalk, or their own photo), add their own video message and a personal message, and type the address. The card is printed and mailed. The person who gets it scans the QR code and points their phone at the postcard: the picture comes alive, and under the card, like the artist's clip under a Blind Canvas painting, a black strip says FROM and the sender's name, with their video playing under it, with sound. Tap the video to pause it. The small picture on the back works too. Without a camera, "Watch it here" shows the same thing on the screen.

The six steps: 1. pick a picture (or upload your own), 2. write the message, 3. record or upload the front video, 4. record or upload a back video (optional; it plays under the card when they scan the back, otherwise the front video plays there too), plus the small picture on the back, 5. who it's going to: "Mail it for me" or "I'll print it myself" (their name goes on the front as To: unless that box is unticked), 6. from you (the name on the FROM strip). Printing it yourself saves the postcard (so its QR code works) and downloads the print files; anything left blank prints as empty lines to write on by hand. No order or payment is made for those yet.

- `magicpostcard/index.html`: the page and the "Make your own" portal, with a live preview of the front and back.
- `magicpostcard/postcard.js`: draws the postcard. The preview, the print files and the picture the camera looks for all come from it.
### Locations, Events and For Businesses

Three example pages, linked from the top of the Magic Postcards page, each with subtabs:
- `magicpostcard/locations.html`: every place card by region (Northeast, South, Midwest, Southwest, West & Pacific). Tap a card to watch it come alive; "Make this card" opens the maker with it chosen (`./?t=<template id>#make`).
- `magicpostcard/events.html`: occasion cards (Birthdays, Holidays, Weddings, New Year, Graduation, Thank You, New Baby). For now they use our place pictures with occasion effects. To add a designed occasion card: put its picture in `stock/` (and a 240 px copy in `stock/thumbs/`), then add an entry to `stock/catalog.json` with `"category": "event"`, `"occasion"` (which subtab it goes in), `"greetText"` (the small script line, e.g. "Happy Birthday") and `"place"` (the big title, e.g. "Pete!"), plus a `live` block (effects: `confetti`, `snow`, `hearts`, `fireworks`, and everything the places use). The maker's step 1 has Locations and Events tabs.
- `magicpostcard/business.html`: one fully produced example (The Sea Glass Hotel, a made-up hotel: the Asbury card with each guest's name, an offer on the back, the hotel logo to scan, and a video under the card), then subtabs with ideas for each industry. "Let's make yours" goes to the contact page, or to `bookingLink` in `site-config.js` if set.

Every card's greeting line is now editable in the maker ("Greetings from", "Happy Holidays from", or none).

### Opened emails and video replies

When someone opens a postcard for the first time, the sender gets an email ("Pete just opened your Magic Postcard!"). The person who got it can record a video reply, or a voice reply if they're camera-shy (a big record button right on the page, or upload a voice memo). It goes privately to the sender: they get an email and watch it on `magicpostcard/replies.html` (Your replies). Opening your own card on the device you ordered it from, or while logged in to your own account, doesn't count. Senders can turn either off in step 6. Each postcard takes up to 10 replies of up to about a minute (50 MB). If you ran `postcard-replies-setup.sql` before voice replies were added, run it again (it's safe).

Setup (once):
1. In Supabase, open SQL Editor and run all of `setup/postcard-replies-setup.sql` (after `postcard-setup.sql`).
2. Emails: make a free account at https://resend.com. Under Domains, add `artalivestudio.com` and add the DNS records it shows you in Cloudflare (DNS → Records). When it says Verified, create an API key.
3. In Supabase, open Edge Functions, deploy a new function with the editor, name it `postcard-events`, and paste all of `setup/postcard-events.ts`. In its settings, turn **off** "Verify JWT" (the people scanning postcards don't have accounts).
4. In Edge Functions → Secrets, add `RESEND_API_KEY` (the key from step 2). Optional: `EMAIL_FROM` (default `Art Alive <postcards@artalivestudio.com>`) and `SITE_URL` (default `https://artalivestudio.com`).

Without the Resend key, nothing is emailed, but replies are still saved and shown on Your replies.

- `magicpostcard/sound.js` and `magicpostcard/stock/sounds/`: soundscapes. A place can have a 16-second sound loop (`"sound"` in the catalog) that plays in step with its animation, on the page and through the camera, and gets quieter while a video message is talking. One sound button turns all of it on or off. Asbury Park's: surf, gulls and the boardwalk crowd by day, a shimmer as the lights come on, a distant band at night, and each firework's whistle, boom and crackle right when it bursts. Sound files are 17 seconds: the last half second of the loop, the 16-second loop, then its first half second.
- `magicpostcard/animate.js`: "Make it move for me". When someone uses their own photo, it brings the photo to life with the Creator Studio's own effects (Glow, Shimmer, Sway, golden hour and night). It finds the sky, the bright spots and the greenery by itself. Three styles: Day to night, Fireworks night, and Sparkle and glow. It's drawn live on the phone, so nothing extra is uploaded.
- `magicpostcard/play.html`: what the QR code opens. Visitors need no account.
- `magicpostcard/stock/`: the place pictures to choose from, listed in `stock/catalog.json` (one entry per place: title, state, city, the place name printed on the card ("Greetings from ARIZONA": the state for a state card, the city for a city card), a one-line blurb, and how it comes alive). The portal shows them with a search box and a state picker, so it grows to every state.
  - **Live places (the default):** a square picture `stock/<id>.jpg` (1024 px or bigger) plus a 240 px copy in `stock/thumbs/<id>.jpg`, and a `live` block in the catalog. No video: Art Alive effects run live on the phone. The `live` block says where the sky and water are, where the sun or a lighthouse lamp sits, where mist drifts, and how the light changes over the loop (golden hour, dusk, stars, sunrise). All settings are explained at the top of `animate.js`. Each postcard keeps a copy of its settings, so editing the catalog never changes cards already sent.
  - **Video places:** like Asbury Park (a 16-second loop made from our effects with hand-tuned lights): the picture plus its animation as an MP4 and a WebM with the same name, and `"video"` in the catalog. An animation must keep the picture exactly in place, because it plays right on top of the printed picture.
  - Live places: all 50 states (plus Asbury Park), each turning from day to a moonlit night with twinkling stars and shooting stars. Effects a place can use: moon and its path on water, Milky Way, northern lights, lens flare, glowing surf, swells, lighthouse beam, warm lit windows, lanterns and boat lights (reflected in water), car headlights along a road, fireflies (or synchronous fireflies), mist, moonbeams through trees, a flowing waterfall, heat lightning, autumn leaves, birds, cloud shadows. Next: two city cards per state (place = the city name).
- `magicpostcard/samples/`: the Asbury Park sample's print files (front and an empty back) and its share picture.

**Switching on ordering (once):**

1. In Supabase, open SQL Editor, then run `setup/postcard-setup.sql` (after the other setup files). It lets projects be postcards, keeps addresses in their own private table, and makes sure postcards never use up a plan's published pieces.
2. Payment (optional): in Stripe, make a Payment Link for one postcard. Under "After payment", redirect to `https://artalivestudio.com/magicpostcard/?paid=1`. Paste it into `stripeLinks.postcard` in `site-config.js`, and put the price in `postcardPrice` (for example `'$4.99'`). With no link, orders are saved without payment, so you can take them by hand at first.

**Filling an order:**

1. In Supabase, open Table Editor, then `postcard_orders`. Each row is one postcard with its address and its `code`.
2. Open `https://artalivestudio.com/magicpostcard/play.html?c=CODE&print=1` and press the two print-file buttons. You get the front and back at 6 × 4 in with 1/8 in bleed, at 300 dpi.
3. Send them to your print-and-mail service with the address (services like Lob and PostGrid print the address on the back for you), then set the row's `status` to `printed` or `mailed`.

## Customer accounts (Supabase)

Customers log in with an email link (no password) and save their animations and games to **My projects**.

- Settings are in `site-config.js`: the Supabase Project URL and **publishable** key. Never put the secret key in any file here.
- One-time setup: in Supabase, open **SQL Editor → New query**, paste everything from `setup/supabase-setup.sql`, and press **Run**.
- To make accounts invite-only, set `openSignup: false` in `site-config.js` and invite people in Supabase under **Authentication → Users → Invite**.
- Pages: `login.html` (log in), `projects.html` (My projects). The Studio and Game Maker each have a **Save to my account** button.

### Unfinished work stays on the customer's device

Nobody loses their work to a closed tab, a refresh or a trip to the camera app. Nothing leaves their phone or computer until they save or order.

- **Creator Studio:** saves itself in the browser as they work, and offers the draft back next time.
- **Game Maker:** once their own picture is in, the unsaved game is kept on the device. Next visit it asks **Bring it back** or **Start fresh**. Saving to the account clears it. It is kept for 7 days at most.
- **Magic Postcard maker:** the card in progress (words, choices, photo, videos and the address) is kept on the device and comes back when they return, with a **Start over** button. Ordering clears it. It is kept for 7 days at most.

### Sign-in codes (optional, worth doing for phones)

On a phone, the link in the sign-in email sometimes opens in a different browser from the one the customer was making in (the Gmail app does this). They are then signed in over there, and their unsaved work is not. A code avoids it: they type the code on the page they are already on, and go straight back to their work.

1. In Supabase, open **Authentication**, then **Emails** (in older dashboards: **Email Templates**). Open **Magic Link**, add a line such as `Or type this code on the page: {{ .Token }}` and save. Do the same in **Confirm signup**.
2. In `site-config.js`, add this line under `openSignup`: `emailCode: true,`
3. Commit and push, then try it once with your own email: the "Check your email" screen now also has a box for the code.

Until you do both steps nothing changes: people sign in with the link, as before. The code works for one hour.

## Publishing (links and QR codes)

- One-time setup: in Supabase **SQL Editor → New query**, paste everything from `setup/publish-setup.sql`, and press **Run** (after `supabase-setup.sql`).
- Creators press **Publish** in the Studio, the Game Maker or My projects. They get a link like `artalivestudio.com/p/?c=abcd2345` and a QR code to download and print.
- Visitors need no account. Games play at that link; animations open in the Studio player, where **Point at the picture** puts the living version on the real picture through the phone camera.
- Saving a published project updates what visitors see. **Unpublish** hides it; publishing again keeps the same link and QR code.

## Collections ("50 images, 1 QR code")

- One-time setup: run `setup/collections-setup.sql` in Supabase's SQL Editor (after the other two).
- In **My projects**, press **New collection**, name it, tick up to 50 pieces, and save. Each piece is published automatically.
- Press **Publish** on the collection for one QR code. Visitors scan it, press **Point at a picture**, and the camera works out which piece they are looking at. There is also a list to choose from, for anyone who prefers it.
- The camera tells pieces apart by their pictures, so very similar-looking pieces are harder to tell apart.

## Stats (how people enjoy each piece)

- One-time setup: run `setup/analytics-setup.sql` in Supabase's SQL Editor (after the publishing and collections setups). Until then the site works as before and simply counts nothing.
- In **My projects**, every live piece and collection has a **Stats** link: visits each day, how many turned on the camera and found the picture, photos taken and shared, the time of day people visit, phones vs computers, time zones and languages.
- Only the creator who published a piece can see its stats. Nothing that identifies a visitor is kept (no names, emails or IP addresses), and browsers set to "do not track" aren't counted.

## Snow globes (artalivestudio.com/snowglobe)

- One-time setup: run `setup/snowglobe-setup.sql` in Supabase's SQL Editor (after `postcard-setup.sql`).
- Anyone can make one: take or choose a photo or video, fit it in the circle, and (for a video) pick the still. Art Alive suggests a sharp, bright frame. Add the words at the top, a name or a logo for the base (shown in gold), and a base colour.

## Pet snow globes (artalivestudio.com/petglobe)

Anyone can put their pet in a snow globe and share a link. **No account needed**, to make one or to open one.

- **Making one** goes a screen at a time, and the globe is not on the page until the end, so nothing is in the way of scrolling. **Step 1**, choose a photo: the pet is cut out right on their phone or computer (the photo itself never leaves it). **Step 2**, on the same screen: Art Alive zooms in on the head and they tap the eyes and nose, so the pet blinks, watches the snow, twitches its ears and sneezes at a snowflake; then **Next** (or **Skip this step**). **Step 3**, on a screen of its own: a name for the plaque and words for the glass, then **Next**. Only then does the globe appear, with the pet in it, a full-width **Shake the snow globe** button right under it, and under that **Step 4**, the link to share. Two small links at the bottom go back to change the name and words, or the photo and the dots.
- **Sharing:** **Get a link to share** saves only the cutout picture, the name, the words and the three taps, and gives a link like `artalivestudio.com/petglobe/?g=abcd2345`. **Share** opens the phone's share sheet (or the share panel on a computer).
- **Opening one:** the link shows the globe straight away, with a **Make one with your pet** button above it and the full-width **Shake the snow globe** button close under it (no words in between, so the button is in view without scrolling). Shaking the phone stirs the snow (iPhones ask permission the first time); so does a tap on the globe, a sideways drag, or that button. A swipe up or down that starts on the globe scrolls the page. There's a small **Report** link.
- **Link preview:** when a pet globe link is texted or posted it shows `petglobe/share.jpg` (and, where an app plays them, the 8-second film `petglobe/share.mp4`): Little Guy, the cat the first pet globe was made for, in his globe, with "Happy Holidays from your loved ones" and "Shake your phone!". It is in the same style as the Ora holiday cards in `play/share/`. Every pet globe link shows this same card, whoever's pet is inside. To change its words or picture, edit `petglobe/share-source.html` (the card as a page; how to turn it back into the picture and the film is written at the top of that file), then raise `?v=1` to `?v=2` in the preview tags at the top of `petglobe/index.html` so apps fetch the new one.
- **Accounts are optional:** under the link there's a small "save it to a free account" option, for people who want to change it later and keep it for good. Links made without an account last a year.
- **Limits:** one person can make 15 an hour, and 3,000 can be made a day in total. Three different people reporting a globe hides it and removes its picture. Reported globes are in Table Editor → `petglobe_reports`.
- The cutout uses the small U²-Net model (`petglobe/u2netp.onnx`, Apache 2.0 licence, free for paid products) running in the browser with ONNX Runtime Web.

**One-time setup:**

1. In Supabase, open **SQL Editor → New query**, paste everything from `setup/petglobe-setup.sql`, and press **Run**.
2. In Supabase, open **Edge Functions**, deploy a new function with the editor, name it `petglobe`, and paste all of `setup/petglobe.ts`. In its settings, turn **off** "Verify JWT" (people making and opening globes don't have accounts). No secrets to add.

## Snowman snow globe: a 3D figure and a game (artalivestudio.com/snowglobe/snowman.html)

A first version, not linked from any page yet. The Ora snowman (made in Claude Design, with the white Ora logo on his blue hat) stands inside the globe, waving, with snow falling behind and in front of him. Drag to turn him, tap him and he hops. The whole bottom of the page is one big SHAKE button: the only button with words on it. A small round **Sound** button turns all sound off or on (see "Sound on or off" below), and opposite it a small round button with a trophy opens the top scores.

- **His hello:** one second after the globe opens (after "Tap to start", because a browser lets no page make sound before a tap), he says in Ben's voice: "Happy holidays, Ora Family! Every snowflake is different. Bring the right ones together and you can really spell something special." His coal smile opens and closes with the words. Nothing stops him: not a shake, and not the game, which begins as soon as the snow settles whether he has finished or not (he goes on speaking through the zoom into the logo and into the game). While he speaks in the globe, a screen reader reads nothing out over him; once the game is on, the game's own words are read out as usual. The recording is `snowglobe/figures/ora-snowman/hello.mp3` ("Sewall Ave 3" with the quiet moment before the first word cut to half a second and nothing else changed: the sound was not re-encoded); `hello.js` beside it holds the words and the opening of his mouth, 50 times a second, measured from the recording (a new recording needs a new measurement); `snowglobe/speak.js` plays a line and moves the mouth.
- **Record your voice, and Share: taken off the page.** Until 16 November 2026 the page had two more buttons. "Record your voice: make the snowman talk!" came after a game: the visitor spoke for up to 12 seconds and the snowman said it back, his mouth moving to their words (the recording stayed in the page and was sent nowhere). Share opened the phone's share sheet with the page's link. Ben asked for both to go, so that SHAKE is the only button. Nothing else in the site was removed: `snowglobe/speak.js` can still record (`createRecorder`) and `snowglobe/sharelink.js` still does the sharing for the other globes, so either can come back; the page as it was is in the site's history on GitHub (`snowglobe/snowman.html` before this change). The link still shows its picture, its title and its film when it is texted (see "Link preview").
- **The game ("Build the Snowman": about a minute, and then the ride):** shake the phone, press SHAKE, swipe across the globe or tap its base. As the snow settles the view zooms into the logo on his hat until the logo fills the screen on Ora blue, then zooms back out of the logo into the game, which fills the screen. The way back to the globe is the same: into the logo, then out of it. (With "reduce motion" switched on in the phone's settings, it fades through the logo instead of zooming.) The game begins with **only his head**, in its hat, sitting on the snow, and he sets off walking: the wood moves past behind him. Slide him left and right (a finger, the **left and right arrow buttons** beside JUMP, the tilt of the phone, or the arrow keys), **all the way to both sides of the screen**, to catch what falls, and press **JUMP** to hop over what lies in his way. When the phrase is complete he is finished off (any snow, arm, glove, button or scarf still missing arrives), **the big gift comes down, and he rides the snowmobile that was in it** (see "The big gift" below). When the ride is over the snowflakes spell the phrase across the sky, then the view goes back through the logo to the globe with the snow flying. Nobody loses.
- **Snowflakes build him:** every snowflake caught is snow for the snowman (a small one counts 1, a big one 2). First a second ball grows under his head and gets bigger (10 of snow); then a third ball grows under that (14 more); after that he grows a little bigger all over (8 more). **As he gets taller the view draws back** (he is drawn smaller: whole, he stands a third of the screen tall, where before it was two fifths), so the sky above him stays tall and there is room over his hat for a jump.
- **Letters spell the phrase:** only the letters that are needed fall, one at a time and in order (there are no wrong letters any more). The phrase is at the top, under the icicles: a word on each row on a phone, all on one row on a wide screen. The next letter pulses there, and each caught letter flies up to its place and turns gold. A missed letter falls again.
- **Gifts:** every few seconds a red gift with a gold bow falls. Caught, it bursts open and what was in it flies to its place on him, in this order: an arm, his other arm, his scarf, a glove, his other glove, two coal buttons, two more buttons (seven gifts in all). An arm or a button comes only when its ball is big enough to wear it, the scarf only when his middle ball is done, and a glove only when its arm is there (if a hit has just made a ball too small, or taken it, the gift is full of snow instead). Until its glove comes, an arm is a bare twig.
- **Icicles:** they grow along the top of the screen and tremble. One falls for every three letters caught (after the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th letter of HAPPY HOLIDAYS; never after the last letter). It is the one that wobbles more than the rest and glows warm (with "reduce motion" on, nothing wobbles: it only glows): it is always one that hangs over where he can stand, right over the third letter as that letter comes down, and when the letter is caught it shakes hard for about a second and a third, then drops (a mark on the snow shows where). If it lands on him it knocks some snow off (4): his newest ball gets smaller, but an icicle never takes a whole ball. A new icicle grows in its place.
- **Walking and jumping:** he walks forward all through the round, and things lie in his way on the snow: first a fallen branch, then two campfires for every branch, one thing every four and a half to seven seconds (about eight in a round). They come along the snow from the right at walking pace (each one starts a second's walk beyond the right side of the screen). A jump lasts about a second and goes about a sixth of the screen high. What counts is the moment the thing reaches him: if he is in the air then, higher than it is, he has cleared it (even if he comes down on it afterwards); if not, he has run into it. So a jump at any time in the second before it arrives will do; the JUMP button lights up, and two notes ring, about half a second before. Nothing new sets out while an icicle is loose or coming down: one thing at a time.
- **The buttons:** a row at the bottom of the screen, in the snow: a **left arrow**, **JUMP**, a **right arrow**. Hold an arrow and he slides that way (gently at first, then as fast as with an arrow key); a short tap is one step, a fifth of the way across. JUMP, the space bar, the up arrow and W all jump, and so does a second finger anywhere on the screen while the first one steers. They are real buttons, named Left, Jump and Right for screen readers; the gaps between them let a touch through to the game.
- **The branch:** a thick fallen limb lying right across his path, from where he walks to the buttons, where its broken end faces us (pale wood and its rings); snow lies along the top of it and broken twigs stand up out of it. (The snow path is a little deeper than it was, so that it shows.) If he does not clear it, **it trips him and takes his bottom ball**, whichever ball that is just now: whole, he loses his bottom ball (and any size he had gained on top of it); with two balls he loses his middle and is a head again; a bare head has nothing to lose. The ball stays behind at the branch and slumps into a heap of snow, and the rest of him **rolls forward along the snow, once over**, a little way along the screen while the ground rushes by, and comes up again. He cannot jump while he rolls (nine tenths of a second), and nothing on the ground can trip or burn him until he is up again. What the gifts brought is never lost: his arms, gloves and scarf wait while he is only a head and are back as soon as his middle is, and the buttons come back with their ball. With "reduce motion" on he does not spin: he drops a ball and carries on.
- **The campfire** burns with the fire from 8 GATES (the one made for the braziers of Luxor, `engine/fire.js` there), drawn flat for this game in `snowglobe/hazards.js` with the same recipe and the same numbers: tongues of flame that rise off the coals, narrow to a point, lean in the gusts and die out red; sparks that fly up out of it; a steady body behind the tongues; a soft glow round it; a warm pool of light on the snow; and the same four colours. It trails a little to the left, the way the air goes past him as he walks. With "reduce motion" on it is the steady body alone, as the original is from far away. If he does not clear it, it melts him a little (5 of snow; the same rule as an icicle: his newest ball gets smaller, but a campfire never takes a whole ball). **And it shows why:** for about a second and a half the end of his scarf (once he has it), the top of his hat and, only just, the tip of his nose burn with that same fire (the one on his nose is small on purpose), then go out with a wisp of smoke. The flames sit exactly on him as he walks, turns his head and jumps: the figure reports where those three points are in each picture it draws (`marks`, in `snowglobe/figures/ora-snowman.js` and `snowglobe/globe3d.js`). With "reduce motion" on they stand still and there is no smoke. How long they burn and how big each is are two lines near the top of `snowglobe/snowgame.js` (`SINGE`, `FLAMES`).
- **The moving wood:** behind him the winter night is in layers (`winterScenery()` in `snowglobe/globe3d.js`: the same sky, moon, hills and pines as the still picture in the globe). The sky, moon and stars keep still; the two rows of hills and three rows of pines go by at their own speeds, far things slowest; drifts in the snow and his own track go by under his feet. With "reduce motion" on, the wood keeps still (only the snow under his feet and the things in his way move, because the game needs them to).
- **Sunshine:** switched off for now. One line at the top of `snowglobe/snowgame.js` (`SUNSHINE = false`) brings it back as it was: twice in the phrase, just after a letter is caught, a ray comes down where he is standing, warns for about a second and a half, then shines and melts him a little if he is still in it.
- **The big gift: the snowmobile** (added 17 November 2026). The moment the phrase is spelled, a big red box with a gold bow comes down and lands in the snow ahead of him; he goes to stand beside it; it shakes, quicker and quicker, and bursts open in a puff of snow. In the puff he is on the **Ora snowmobile**: Ora blue, the white Ora logo on each side of its hood, a round headlight on its nose. **On the snowmobile the two buttons beside JUMP are SLOWER and FASTER** (two arrowheads each): they bring it back and forward along the snow (see Free to move, below). **JUMP stays, and a tap on the screen is a jump too** (so are the space bar, the up arrow and W). The view is from further back than on foot, so that most of the screen is the way ahead: on a wide screen the snowmobile is a little under half the play area wide, a quarter of the way across. **On a phone held upright it is seen from further back still** (Ben, 17 November: "zoom out to give more room for the upcoming track on phones portrait"): the snowmobile is about a third of the screen wide and, left alone, rides close to the left edge, so that about two of its lengths of track show ahead of it, and everything on the ride (the campfires, the branches, the coins, its jump, its speed across the screen) is smaller by as much. Something coming along is in view for nearly two seconds before it arrives, where it was one. Its jump is never so high that the top coins would be up among the score and the light bar (on a wide, not very tall screen the snowmobile is made a little smaller to leave that room). A phone held on its side is the one screen too low for that: there the jump is a low hop, and the top coins pass behind the score for a moment.
- **The ride** is through the same wood, faster than his walk (one and a half times as fast at first). Things come along the snow one after another: a line of **Ora coins** to ride through; a **campfire** or a **branch** to hop over, with the path of a good jump marked out over it in coins; **coins up high**, four in a row, which his head only reaches near the top of a jump (a jump at the right moment gets all four); or the **top coins**, five in a row, each with a ring of light round it, which are just out of reach of an ordinary jump (see Free to move, below); or a **ramp** (see Ramps, below). And **icicles fall**, as they did while he walked (see Skill decides, below). What counts is the same as on foot: the moment the thing reaches the middle of the snowmobile, if he is in the air and higher than it is, he is over it (100 points). The JUMP button lights up and two notes ring when it is time: a little earlier than on foot (0.85 seconds before), and any jump from then until a quarter of a second before the thing arrives clears it.
- **Free to move: Slower and Faster** (17 November, late evening; Ben: "currently instead of feeling that the snow mobile adds something fun it seems cramped and limiting", and "when pressing the forward button it needs to have more of a reaction AND the background should move more quickly too"). The snowmobile is no longer fixed near the left edge. **Hold the Faster button (where the Right button was; or the Right arrow key, or D) and it surges forward across the screen; the Slower button (the Left arrow key, or A) brings it back.** A short tap moves it a third of the way. A finger that slides on the screen moves it too, as on foot (a finger that keeps still and lifts is a jump; so is a second finger while the first is steering). It can be brought forward until its nose is four fifths of the way across a phone's screen. **How far forward it is, is how fast it is going**: at the back, its ordinary speed; right forward, 60% faster. (This is what the Faster button of earlier that evening has become; Ben then: "allow the player to go faster by pushing the RIGHT arrow and time the jumps to get more coins up higher".) **A push forward is answered at once**: the snowmobile lunges ahead with its nose up; a burst of snow flies from its track, and the spray behind it is thicker; white streaks of air rush past (they show in the dark too; not with "reduce motion" on); the engine, which purrs at the back, is louder and higher; two quick low notes sound; and **the wood behind goes by faster**: on the ride the trees pass one and a half times as fast as before for the same speed (twice, on a phone), and faster again the further forward he is. **The faster it is going when it jumps, the higher it jumps**: up to a third higher. That is what the top coins want: speed, and then a jump at the right moment (within about a fifth of a second for all five). The first time they come, the words say so: "Top coins! Hold the arrow, then jump". Going faster makes the whole ride go by faster: the ride's seconds are **seconds of track**, so what comes along, the dark stretches, the lamp running down and the points for riding all come that much quicker. What being forward costs is time to see what is coming: right forward on a phone, a campfire is on the screen for well under a second before it arrives (the notes ring and JUMP lights in time all the same). Nobody has to use it: left alone the snowmobile rides at the back, and the whole ride can be ridden there.
- **GOOD TIDINGS: snowflakes fall again** (17 November, late evening; Ben: "lets add snowflakes falling again. SPELLING GOOD TIDINGS. AND pressing the speed up button will help the player catch some snowflakes that could be missed otherwise"). On the ride the phrase at the top of the screen is GOOD TIDINGS, and its letters come down as snowflakes, one at a time, as the letters of HAPPY HOLIDAYS did: the same snowflake, the same notes, the letter that is needed marked at the top. **Most come down ahead of where the snowmobile is** (about three in four): it has to be brought forward to be under them, which is faster; the rest come down behind it. A letter is caught by being under it as it comes down to him, on the snow or in the air: 50 points, as on foot. One that reaches the snow is missed and comes again; the first letter, and one after two misses, comes down nearer. Each time GOOD TIDINGS is whole there are 200 points, the words "GOOD TIDINGS! Spell it again", and **the lamp gains a quarter of its light**; then it begins again, and its letters fall a little quicker each time. The first letter comes six seconds in, when the first ramp has been ridden ("Catch GOOD TIDINGS! Arrows move him"). By ear, a letter rings from the side it is on and higher when he is under it, as on foot; screen readers are told "T, ahead: go faster", "behind: drop back" or "right above you". When the ride is over the snowflakes spell the phrase of the round (HAPPY HOLIDAYS), as before. On the score card the letters of both phrases are together under Letters.
- **Ramps** (added 17 November, later the same evening; Ben: "will it be more challanging when we add the ramps?"). A ramp is a wedge about as long as the snowmobile and a third as high. It is **high contrast against the snow** (Ben, the same evening: "LETS make the ramps high contrast against the snow"): its side is dark Ora blue with a darker edge, three bold gold arrowheads on it point up the slope, the slope is capped in white (which shows against the dark wood behind), and at its top is a coral flag edged in white. He rides up it, and when its top has gone by under his skis it throws him into a jump **by itself**: about as high as an ordinary jump, but from the top of the ramp. The path of that jump is marked out in five coins, so someone who never presses anything gets them. **JUMP pressed near the top of the ramp makes the jump a good deal bigger** ("BIG AIR!"): over the last third of the ramp (about a third of a second's ride) the JUMP button lights and the two notes ring; a press anywhere in that time sets the big jump, which goes off at the top; and a press up to a fifth of a second after the ramp has thrown him still counts. Over the path are four coins with rings of light that only the big jump reaches, or speed (the Faster button makes every jump higher, a ramp's too). Fast, with JUMP at the top, he goes as high as the screen allows: his hat stops just short of the light bar. JUMP pressed lower down the ramp is an ordinary jump from where he is. **The first thing on the ride is a ramp, within its first few seconds** (Ben, 17 November, late evening: "lets make more ramps and sooner"; the words: "A ramp! JUMP at the top for big air"), and there are four in the free half minute. After the free seconds about one thing in three that comes along is a ramp (it was one in five), and a ramp may have a campfire under the path of its jump (any jump from the ramp carries him over it: 100 points), or, more and more often as the ride goes on (one ramp in five at first, three in five after four minutes), **two things after it**: one under the path and another just where the ramp's own jump comes down. Only the big jump, or speed, clears both ("Long jump! Press JUMP at the top"). **That is where ramps make the ride harder**; everywhere else they are a help and a treat. In the dark a ramp shows from the first, dimly (its white edge, its gold arrowheads and its flag catch what light there is), so that JUMP can be got ready for it.
- **Light and dark.** The ride is about light. **It begins in the dark** (Ben: "so player gets to experience it right away in the snow mobile and its not confusing when it happens later"): as the puff of snow clears, the wood is dark and the headlight is on. Stretches of the wood are **dark**: the screen goes dark except where there is light. The **headlight** throws a beam ahead and lights the snow in front of the skis: a branch lying in the dark cannot be seen, or heard coming, until the beam reaches it. A **campfire** shows by its own light from as far off as the screen goes, and lights the snow and the air round it (and a branch or a coin near it). Coins glint in the dark. The snowmobile and the snowman are never lost in it. **Every coin makes the beam bigger**: it reaches further, so what is coming is seen, and rung, sooner. The small bar under the score, with a lamp at its left end, shows how much light there is (it turns coral and pulses when the lamp is nearly out).
- **The first 20 seconds are free** (it was 30 until 18 November). For the first 20 seconds of track nothing can go wrong: the lamp does not run down, and running into a campfire, or being hit by an icicle, is only a knock. What comes along in the first half minute comes in a set order (`FIRST`), so that everything is met once. In the first dark (the first 13 seconds): **a ramp at once** (it throws him by itself, so nothing has to be known yet), coins, another ramp, and a campfire that glows from as far off as the screen goes. In the moonlight: **the first icicle**, 16 seconds in (nothing comes along the snow in its turn, so that its words can be read: "Icicle! Move out from under it"), and then the top coins. In a second dark (from second 23 to second 35½): a ramp, a campfire, a ramp, and the first branch, which only the headlight shows. The first letter of GOOD TIDINGS comes six seconds in. So someone who does not play games still has the ramp, the coins, the dark, a campfire and an icicle before anything counts.
- **After that, skill decides how long the ride lasts** (18 November; Ben: "Can we make it so a person has to be really skilled to keep going? ALSO so that the game ends quicker for people who are just dabblers as players? So they finish faster and see thier high scores?", and: "LETS bring back the falling icecycles that drain the lamp energy, lets have hitting any obstacle take significant lamp energy, AND lets give less lamp energy per coin but keep the points the same"). From the 20th second the lamp slowly runs down, a little faster for every second ridden. **Running into a campfire or a branch takes a third of the lamp** (it was about a fifth; "Bump! Your light is dimmer"; for a moment after it nothing else can catch him; a campfire also sets his scarf, hat and nose alight for a moment, as on foot). **Icicles fall again**: every eight seconds or so at first, every four and a half at last, the icicle that hangs nearest over the snowmobile shakes for a little over a second and then drops. While it shakes it glows and ticks (higher and louder while he is under it), the words say "Icicle! Move out from under it" (after the first two, just "Icicle!"), a mark shows on the snow where it will land, and a pale column stands over the mark, because the snowmobile hides it; in the dark all three glow. He has to be moved out from under it, forward or back. **One that comes down on him takes a quarter of the lamp** ("Icicle! Your light is dimmer"); one that comes down in the moment after a bump breaks on him without harm. Early in the ride an icicle does not begin to shake while a campfire, a branch or a ramp is about to reach him: one thing at a time. Later they come as they will. **A coin charges the lamp half as much as it did** (100 coins fill an empty lamp; it was 50) and scores the same 10 points. Each GOOD TIDINGS still gives a quarter of the lamp, which is now worth 25 coins. All the while the ride gets a little faster, the dark stretches get longer and the moonlight between them shorter, things come closer together, and now and then two come one close behind the other. **When the lamp is out the ride is over**: the snowmobile comes to a stop ("The lamp is out. What a ride!"), turns to face us, and the snowflakes spell the phrase. So there is no set end. Worked out by playing the game's numbers 300 times over for each kind of player (in seconds of track; right forward they go by in five eighths of the time): someone who presses nothing rides for just under half a minute (it was a minute); someone new to games about 35 seconds (it was a minute and a half); a fair player three quarters of a minute (it was nearly two minutes); a good one a minute and a half; a very good one two minutes; an expert two and three quarters; and a ride without one mistake, with every letter and every top coin, lasts as long as it did, about three and a third minutes of track (some two and a half minutes by the clock, ridden well forward), because by then the lamp runs down faster than all the coins and letters there are can charge it. When the lamp goes out the snowmobile stops where it may ride, however fast it was being brought forward. (That last limit is there because the best score there can be has to stay under the top-score list's limit of 20,000.)

- **How the sounds were made ready** (the recordings as Ben made them are not on the site; the scripts that did this, and how to run them again, are in `setup/snowman-sounds/`): each was cut to its moment and cleared of the rumble a phone cannot play. The three small-snowflake takes came as a faint, even hiss, so each was given the shape of a pat (a quick rise, gone in a fifth of a second) and kept to the middle range. The letter chime came as one held chord, as loud at its end as at its start: it was given the shape of something struck, and its very top was turned down so that it is bright, not shrill. The finale came in A flat major, a semitone away from the notes the game rings at that moment: it is played a major third higher, which makes it C major, the game's key (it also reaches its height just as the phrase finishes forming). The trip's plop came a second and a half after its knock: the middle of the tumble was shortened so that the plop lands as he comes up, nine tenths of a second after. The hiss after the campfire's "fwoomp" and the scatter after the icicle's crack were brought up so that a phone plays them. The button tap is the clearest of the four taps in its recording. The walk came as five crunches: they were cut apart, so the game can put one under each step. The winter night keeps the range between 260 and 2,400 Hz, with six sharp ticks rounded off. The campfire's low roar was turned well down and its few loudest pops rounded off, so no single pop is heard coming round. **The snowmobile's fifteen** (`prep_ride.py`, 18 November; Ben made seventeen recordings, "Runway Agent - 17 generations"): the cruising purr and the full-speed hum each became a loop (the purr came even from end to end; of the hum only the first five and a half seconds, which are). The engine's start came with its starter's whir 20 dB under the bang of the engine catching: the whir was brought up by 20 dB and the bang rounded off, so that a phone plays both. The start's low hum and the engine's dying putter were nearly all below 150 Hz, which a phone cannot play: each was given overtones of its own (the bass driven gently into a soft ceiling) before the rumble was taken off. The speeding-up begins a sixth of a second in and is played a quarter faster, so that it rises as fast as the snowmobile crosses the screen. The slowing-down that was asked for came as a steady hum that does not sink: the one the game uses was made from the full-speed hum, played slower and slower. The coin came as one held note (the C three octaves above middle C), as loud at its end as at its start: it was given the shape of something struck. The low-lamp warning and the icicle's creak came almost silent, but clean: they were only brought up. The icicle's smash came dull, more a thud than ice: its top was brought up. The flourish for GOOD TIDINGS came cut off at two seconds: it is let fade. **Not used:** the globe shake (it came almost silent: the globe keeps its own swirl and bells); of the snowmobile's, the "slows down" (above), the wind at speed (it came as a deep rumble, nearly all below 300 Hz, which a phone cannot play), and the long single take of the engine (kept by Ben as a spare). **Not made yet:** the music box for the globe, and his six short spoken lines. - **How loud they are:** every file was written at one loudness, and `snowglobe/sounds.js` has one line (`LOUD`) that says how loud each sound is played, in dB as it will seem on a phone speaker (3 more is a small step, 6 a clear one, and 10 sounds about twice as loud; `MASTER` moves them all). The game's own guiding notes run from about −40 (a letter ticking far to one side) to −23 (a letter caught); each recorded sound is set under the note it goes with. The faintest guiding notes are the low knocks of a branch or a campfire on its way, and what a phone plays of them lies between 450 and 700 Hz: so the two sounds that are there all the time keep clear of it (the winter night is turned down by 7 dB just there, and the steps keep above 700 Hz). This was checked by keeping what the page sends to the loudspeaker during robots' rounds and measuring it: each sound came out within about 1 dB of its figure, and every guiding note could still be heard over the night, his steps and the snowflake pats (in its own narrow band of pitch each stood above them, or level with them). **The snowmobile's engine** goes on all the while he rides, so it too keeps clear of the knocks (turned down by 5 dB between 450 and 700 Hz) and is set low: the purr at −38 (`purr`), the hum right forward at −34.5 (`hum`). Measured the same way on robots' rides: the engine alone came out at about −39; all the recorded sounds of a ride together were at about −33 for most of it and −25 at their loudest moment, with the guiding notes between −38 and −21. Nobody has yet listened to it on a phone but Ben: if the engine is too loud or too soft, `purr` and `hum` are the two figures to change. To change a sound, make its file again (`setup/snowman-sounds/`), replace it under the same name and put its measured loudness in `AS_WRITTEN` in the same file; a loop must keep its length (they are a whole number of MP3 frames long, with 0.3 seconds of the loop repeated before and after it, so the join is good whatever a browser does with the very start of the file).

## Link previews (picture and title when a link is texted or posted)

The main site is one page for every piece, so a small Cloudflare helper ("Worker") answers apps like iMessage with the right picture and title, then sends people on. It's free.

1. In Cloudflare, open **Workers & Pages → Create → Create Worker**. Name it `artalive-share` and press **Deploy**.
2. Press **Edit code**, delete what's there, paste everything from `setup/share-worker.js`, and press **Deploy**.
3. In the Worker, open **Settings → Domains & Routes → Add → Custom domain**, type `share.artalivestudio.com`, and add it. Cloudflare sets up the address and its padlock by itself.
4. In `site-config.js`, set `shareBase: 'https://share.artalivestudio.com/'`, then commit and push.

New links then look like `share.artalivestudio.com/k7m2qx9a`. Old links and printed QR codes keep working.
